Which denied claims are worth fighting for?

When an insurer denies a hospital claim, a billing specialist has to choose: fix the claim and resend it, appeal the decision, or write the money off. An appeal costs 57 to 108 USD of staff time, so appealing everything wastes money and appealing nothing leaves revenue unpaid.

This prototype makes that choice for every denial, shows its reasoning step by step, writes the appeal letter, and hands the cases it should not decide alone to a person.

How a denial moves through the system

  1. Read the denialThe insurer's reason code, the claim, and the notes in the record.
  2. Simple rules firstA typo or a missing number on file needs no AI. Rules settle 57 of 200.
  3. AI reads it twiceTwo separate reads of the record. If they disagree, a person decides.
  4. Compare in dollarsChance of winning times the amount, minus the cost of each action.
  5. Act or hand offTake the best action, or pass it to a person with a recommendation.
  6. Write and check the letterEvery fact must point to a line in the record, then a second AI reviews it.

Product decisions worth noticing

How to read this demo

The 200 denials are synthetic, so each one has a known right answer. That lets every decision be scored in dollars: what the system earned against the best possible action.

Use Show the right answers at the top to hide them and see only what the system saw.

Nothing here calls an AI model. The page replays saved results, so it shows exactly what happened in the test run.

Terms used on this page
Denial
An insurer's refusal to pay a claim, sent with a reason code.
Payer
The insurance company or plan that pays the claim.
Reason code
The standard code for why the claim was denied. CO-50 means "not medically necessary", CO-29 "filed too late", CO-197 "no prior authorization".
Allowed amount
What the payer would pay if the claim were approved.
Fix and resend
Correct the provider's own mistake, such as a wrong ID or missing records, and submit again. Costs about 25 USD.
Appeal
Ask the payer to reverse its decision, with evidence. Costs 57 USD, or 108 USD when clinical review is needed.
Write off
Accept the denial. Right when there is no real basis to recover the money.
Win odds
The chance an appeal succeeds, based on past outcomes for similar denials.
Expected value
What an action is worth on average: chance of success times the amount, minus its cost.
Person decides
A billing specialist makes the call, starting from the system's recommendation. Costs about 15 USD of their time.
Value captured
Dollars the system earned, divided by what the best action on every denial would have earned.
